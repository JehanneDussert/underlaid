## Underlaid v0.3.0 — travel times to everyday places, new site, 2022 census

New site at **https://underlaid.fr** and a new measure: travel times without a car to everyday places, for three ways of getting around. The cumulative score keeps its 4 categories (0 to 4); its figures move slightly with the switch to the 2022 census.

### What changed

**Travel times to everyday places** (information, never scored). For each of the 2,752 neighbourhoods:
- the time to the nearest emergency department, town hall, France Services, CAF, CPAM, France Travail agency, post office, GP, pharmacy, crèche, supermarket, public nursery, primary and secondary schools, police station, community centre, library and park;
- in Paris, also public toilets and drinking fountains;
- the walking time to the nearest heavy-network stop (tram, metro, RER, Transilien).

A travel time is the shorter of walking alone and walking with public transport (Île-de-France Mobilités timetables, Tuesday 13 October 2026, departures 10:00–11:00). The neighbourhood value is the population-weighted median of its inhabited 200 m cells.

There are three ways of getting around:
- without constraint (4.5 km/h);
- slow walking (3.4 km/h);
- wheelchair: 0.8 m/s, no stairs, no streets steeper than 8 % (IGN RGE ALTI 1 m), and only stops and trips marked accessible.

**Second travel-time calculation** (pre-registered on 4 and 5 October 2026):
- same start and end points for every way of getting around;
- remaining cases where the wheelchair time comes out shorter than without constraint: 11 of 62,788 (0.02 %), against 0.8 % before;
- the publication-blocking thresholds were written before the results. Two were crossed, and the decisions taken are recorded in the Method page and in `docs/checks/`.

**Three pre-registered tests, plus one additional test.** The verdicts below remain the official results. The three pre-registered ones were computed with the 2021 census and confirmed with the 2022 census; those that depend on travel times were confirmed with the second calculation.
- Access to care: supported. Neighbourhoods with the fewest resources have harder access to care in Hauts-de-Seine and Val-de-Marne, also at equal density.
- Inclusive mobility: refuted. The loss of access in a wheelchair is concentrated in Paris, where few metro stops are accessible, whatever the neighbourhood's resources.
- Public services: refuted, and reversed. Neighbourhoods with the most resources are more often among the farthest from public services, in Hauts-de-Seine, Seine-Saint-Denis and Val-de-Marne (in all four départements in a wheelchair).
- Additional test (not verifiably pre-registered), households without a car: refuted, and reversed. Neighbourhoods with the most households without a car are clearly less often far from public services in Hauts-de-Seine, Seine-Saint-Denis and Val-de-Marne. Formulated on 3 October 2026 before the calculation, but only in unversioned working notes; its first dated record in the repository is one minute later than the calculation (2022 census). Confirmed with the second travel-time calculation.

**2022 census as the reference** for population, overcrowded homes (new INSEE definition: median 13.5 % → 24.6 %, a change of definition, not a real change) and secondary residences. Income stays 2021, the latest vintage published at neighbourhood level.

**"Highly exposed" = at least 2 of the 3 exposures** (heat, air and noise, energy-inefficient housing) everywhere on the site. Access to care is a separate axis.

**New site:**
- French at the root, English under `/en/`;
- a prerendered page per neighbourhood, whose address contains no street address;
- a map with six themes and filters;
- Method, About, Corrections and Accessibility pages;
- automated accessibility checks (axe) on every page in continuous integration.
